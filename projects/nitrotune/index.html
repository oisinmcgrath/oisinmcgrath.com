NitroTune

Get the fans on your Acer laptop to work like they were meant to. Your laptop is quiet, then it sounds like a hairdryer, all while you're on a zoom call for work. Fix it for good with NitroTune.

Stop noticing the noise, while your laptop maintains a cooler idle.

See What It Is Doing

Temperature, fan speed, memory and battery are now all trackable on screen — readings Linux never showed you.

If Something Goes Wrong

If NitroTune stops, the laptop takes back control of its own fans. If it cannot read the temperature, the fans go up rather than down. Nothing is left to chance.

A word of caution. The fan-curve daemon writes directly to embedded-controller registers that were identified by experiment on one machine. On different hardware those addresses may mean something else entirely. Watch the log for a few minutes before enabling it at boot, and treat the whole project as what it is: a personal tool for one laptop model, shared as-is with no warranty.

Requirements

  • Linux with a recent kernel — developed on Linux Mint 22, kernel 6.8
  • nbfc-linux, for ec_probe
  • nvidia-smi, for GPU temperature on NVIDIA hardware
  • acer_wmi loaded with predator_v4=1, for thermal profiles
  • python3-gi and gir1.2-gtk-3.0, for the tray monitor

On the BIOS this was tested against (V2.06, 08/19/2021), only the balanced thermal profile does anything — the other modes are accepted and then quietly ignored by the firmware. Run acer-profile status to see what yours honours before relying on it.

Getting it

NitroTune is free software. The source is on GitHub, and the repository is the whole distribution — there is no installer, no store listing and no account.
